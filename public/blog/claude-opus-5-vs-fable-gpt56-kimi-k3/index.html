Claude Opus 5 Just Dropped — How It Stacks Up Against Fable 5, GPT-5.6, and Kimi K3

Anthropic just released Claude Opus 5 — and the benchmarks are staggering. It doubles Opus 4.8 on agentic coding, triples every model on novel problem-solving, and delivers near-Fable intelligence at half the price. Here's the full four-way comparison with GPT-5.6 Sol, Kimi K3, and Opus 4.8 — plus the use cases where each model actually wins.

TL;DR — The 60-Second Version

  • What: Claude Opus 5 launched July 24, 2026. Same $5/$25 per million token pricing as Opus 4.8. Half the cost of Fable 5. Available immediately on all Anthropic platforms.
  • Headline numbers: 43.3% Frontier-Bench (vs. Opus 4.8's 18.7% and Fable 5's 33.7%). 30.2% ARC-AGI 3 (next best: GPT-5.6 Sol at 7.8%). 1.5× pass rate on Zapier AutomationBench vs. any other model.
  • vs. Fable 5: Within 0.5% on CursorBench 3.2 at max effort. Surpasses Fable 5 on Frontier-Bench and OSWorld 2.0. Still behind on cybersecurity (Mythos 5 retains that crown).
  • vs. GPT-5.6 Sol: Opus 5 wins agentic coding and novel reasoning. Sol wins terminal operations and context window size (1.5M vs 1M). Pricing is comparable ($5/$25 vs $5/$30).
  • vs. Kimi K3: K3 is free and open-weight at 2.8T parameters — but trails Opus 5 on agentic work and faces a potential US government ban.
  • vs. Opus 4.8: Opus 5 more than doubles performance on Frontier-Bench, triples it on novel problem-solving, and generates 26% fewer tokens for equivalent results.
  • Business takeaway: For most enterprise and SMB workloads, Opus 5 is now the smartest model you can run daily without paying frontier prices. Route Fable 5 for the hardest 5%, Opus 5 for the core 80%, and Sonnet 5 for high-volume work.

Why This Release Matters More Than Another Model Drop

We cover every major model launch on this blog. GPT-5.6. Sonnet 5. Fable 5. And we'll be honest: there's a point where the benchmark-of-the-week cycle starts to feel like noise.

Opus 5 is different. Here's why.

For the past two months, the frontier AI landscape has had a clear structure: Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol sit at the top, trading wins depending on the benchmark, both priced at the premium tier ($10/$50 and $5/$30 respectively). Below them, Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 handled the everyday workload — competent but visibly behind the frontier.

Opus 5 collapses that gap. It performs within striking distance of Fable 5 on coding and knowledge work — and outright beats it on several benchmarks — while staying at the $5/$25 price point. It also introduces a 30.2% score on ARC-AGI 3 that is, frankly, hard to believe: nearly four times the score of GPT-5.6 Sol on a benchmark specifically designed to resist AI gaming.

And it arrives the same week that China's Kimi K3 — a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model — is shaking up the market from the other direction, offering free access to near-frontier intelligence while the White House threatens sanctions.

So: four models. Four different philosophies. Four different price points. Let's break it down.

Opus 5 vs. Fable 5: Do You Still Need the Flagship?

This is the question Anthropic clearly wants enterprises asking — and their answer is nuanced enough to be credible.

Where Opus 5 wins over Fable 5:

  • Frontier-Bench: 43.3% vs 33.7%. Opus 5 is actually better at agentic terminal coding.
  • OSWorld 2.0: Surpasses Fable 5's best result at roughly a third of the cost.
  • Zapier AutomationBench: ~1.5× the pass rate of any other model, even at its lowest effort setting.
  • Price: $5/$25 vs $10/$50. Half the cost per million tokens.

Where Fable 5 still leads:

  • CursorBench peak: At max effort, Fable 5 still edges Opus 5 by ~0.5%.
  • Cybersecurity: Fable 5 (and the restricted Mythos 5) remain ahead on offensive security capabilities.
  • Biology research: Anthropic explicitly acknowledges Opus 5 remains behind Mythos 5 on life sciences evaluations at the frontier, though it improves over Opus 4.8 on every single one.
  • Adaptive thinking maturity: Fable 5's always-on adaptive thinking has been tuned over more deployment time.

The verdict: For 90% of enterprise workloads — software development, document analysis, business automation, customer-facing agents — Opus 5 delivers Fable-class intelligence at Opus pricing. Reserve Fable 5 for the genuinely frontier tasks: novel security research, cutting-edge scientific work, and the rare cases where that last 0.5% of CursorBench matters.

The Kimi K3 Wild Card

And then there's Kimi K3 — the model that has the White House threatening sanctions and Nvidia's CEO begging Washington not to ban it.

Released July 16 by China's Moonshot AI, Kimi K3 is a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model — the largest ever released publicly. And its benchmarks, for a free model, are absurd:

  • Terminal-Bench 2.1: 88.3% — within 0.5% of GPT-5.6 Sol's best score
  • BrowseComp: 91.2% — beats every model tested, including Sol and Fable 5
  • SWE Marathon: 42% — leads all models on long-horizon coding
  • Automation Bench: 30.8% — leads all models on business task completion
  • Pricing: $3/$15 per million tokens on API. Free on kimi.com.

Those numbers are real. Artificial Analysis independently scored K3 at 57 on their Intelligence Index — on par with Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5, just a few points behind Sol and Fable 5.

But here's the context that matters:

Michael Kratsios, the White House's Director of Science and Technology Policy, has publicly accused Moonshot AI of distilling Anthropic's Fable model to build K3 and acquiring banned Nvidia Blackwell GB300 chips through Thailand. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has threatened sanctions and entity-list designations. The Commerce Department has opened a formal investigation. And Axios reports the Trump administration is actively considering banning US companies from hosting Chinese AI models entirely.

Moonshot denies the distillation allegations. Nvidia's Jensen Huang has publicly urged the US to embrace open models rather than ban them, arguing that shutting China out only pushes it to build its own chip ecosystem faster.

Meanwhile, 179 US startups wrote a letter to the White House begging them not to ban foreign open-source models — because they depend on them.

The business calculus: K3 is genuinely impressive and aggressively priced. But building production workflows on a model that could be banned, sanctioned, or restricted at any moment is a risk most enterprises can't afford to take. Use it for experimentation and benchmarking. Don't make it load-bearing.

The Best Use Cases for Each Model

Benchmarks tell you what a model can do. Use cases tell you what it should do. Here's how to route work across these four models:

Claude Opus 5 — Your Daily Driver

  • Complex software engineering: Debugging production codebases, multi-file refactors, architectural decisions. The Frontier-Bench score translates directly to real-world code quality.
  • Autonomous business agents: The Zapier AutomationBench dominance means Opus 5 can handle end-to-end business workflows — flagging at-risk accounts, routing support tickets, generating reports — better than any other model.
  • Novel problem-solving: The ARC-AGI 3 score signals a genuine step forward in handling tasks without established patterns. Strategy analysis, competitive research, creative problem framing.
  • Long-running agent sessions: Mid-conversation tool changes and the new max effort tier make it ideal for multi-hour agent runs that need to adapt their toolset as they work.
  • Cost-sensitive enterprise deployment: Fable-class results at Opus pricing. For teams spending $10K+/month on AI API calls, the savings are material.

Claude Fable 5 — The Frontier Reserve

  • Cutting-edge security research: Via Project Glasswing, with Mythos 5's safeguards. Still the undisputed leader in vulnerability discovery.
  • Hard science problems: Biology, chemistry, structural analysis where that last percentage point of accuracy matters.
  • Maximum-stakes code: Financial systems, safety-critical infrastructure, anywhere a 0.5% accuracy gap has real consequences.

GPT-5.6 Sol — The Shell and Security Specialist

  • DevOps and terminal automation: Terminal-Bench leadership isn't academic — if your agents live in the shell, Sol is faster and more reliable.
  • Web scraping and research agents: Strong BrowseComp scores plus the larger 1.5M context window make it ideal for ingesting and synthesizing large web datasets.
  • Cybersecurity: 96.7% on CTF challenges. Sol is the best publicly available model for security work outside Anthropic's restricted Mythos tier.
  • Multi-agent orchestration: OpenAI's "Ultra" mode coordinates parallel subagent workstreams — powerful for tasks that decompose cleanly into independent subtasks.

Kimi K3 — The Open-Weight Experimenter

  • Self-hosted inference: When the full weights drop (expected July 27), organizations that need air-gapped or on-premise AI will have a frontier-competitive option for the first time.
  • Research and benchmarking: Compare results across models without worrying about API costs. K3 is free on kimi.com.
  • Web research agents: That 91.2% BrowseComp score is the highest of any model tested. For pure web data extraction, K3 leads.
  • Caution: Do not build production pipelines on K3 until the regulatory picture clarifies. The ban risk is real.

The Bigger Picture: We're in the Efficiency Era

Step back from the individual scores and a pattern emerges.

Six months ago, the AI industry was in a pure capability race: who can score highest on the hardest benchmark? That race produced Fable 5, GPT-5.6 Sol, and Mythos 5 — extraordinary models that cost extraordinary amounts to run.

Opus 5 signals the next phase: the efficiency era.

Anthropic isn't just releasing a more capable model. They're releasing a model that's more capable per dollar spent. The entire launch framing — effort ladders, cost-per-task charts, token efficiency metrics — is designed to answer the question enterprises are actually asking: "How do I get frontier results without frontier bills?"

OpenAI is playing the same game with Sol's tiered family (Sol/Terra/Luna). Moonshot is undercutting everyone on price with K3. And Google's Gemini team is likely watching all of this very carefully.

For businesses, this is unambiguously good news. The best AI models are getting cheaper, more efficient, and more practical to deploy at scale. The premium for frontier capability is shrinking. And the infrastructure to route intelligently across models — using the right model for each task at the right effort level — is becoming the differentiator, not the model itself.

Bottom Line

Claude Opus 5 is the best model most businesses should be running right now. Not because it's the absolute best at everything — Fable 5 still leads on pure frontier capability, Sol still owns terminal work, K3 still has the best BrowseComp score. But because it delivers 90% of frontier intelligence at 50% of frontier pricing, with the best novel-reasoning scores we've ever seen from any model.

The Opus 4.8 → Opus 5 jump is the largest single-generation improvement Anthropic has shipped. If you're still running 4.8 in production, upgrading is a no-brainer — same price, vastly better results, fewer tokens burned.

And if you're evaluating K3? It's genuinely impressive. Use it. Test it. But don't build your business on a model that might be sanctioned next week. That's not strategy — it's gambling.

Build the routing layer. Deploy the effort ladder. And stop paying frontier prices for tasks that don't need frontier capability.

That's not just our advice. It's what Anthropic is telling you with Opus 5's entire existence.
